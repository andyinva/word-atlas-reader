Question
========
Can you find groups of words used in Revelation that are taken from Jeremiah 51?

Answer
======
Yes, but only a little. The dataset finds two word groups in Revelation that match Jeremiah 51, and both rest on the same verse, Jeremiah 51:7.

**1. "a golden cup" (Revelation 17:4 and Jeremiah 51:7).**
- This comes from the Revelation Book page, section 4, "Echoes [Revelation] -> other books". It is also in the Section page [Revelation: Babylon and the new Jerusalem], section 4 (row 56).
- The grade cell is blank, so it is not marked as a "quotation". Only a reading of the two passages can say whether it is an allusion or a shared idiom.

**2. "babylon, cup, wine" (Revelation 16:19 and Jeremiah 51:7).**
- This comes from the Chapter page [Revelation 16], section 6, "Kin [Revelation 16]: chapters sharing rare words in any order". Jeremiah 51 is the kin chapter listed.
- It has a score of 38, with 3 shared words, all 3 in order. It was found by English wording.

**Chapter-level pattern.** In section 4b, "Echoes by chapter" (Book page, and the Section page for chapters 17 to 22), Revelation 17's echoes point most to 1 Kings 10 (weight 42), then Daniel 7 (31), then Jeremiah 51 (30). So Jeremiah 51 is a real secondary source for that chapter, but not the main one.

**Caveats**
- I found no Jeremiah 51 rows in section 4e (Septuagint echoes in Greek). One row there cites "Jeremiah 44:13 (Rahlfs 51:13)", but that is KJV Jeremiah 44, not Jeremiah 51. It covers the phrase "with sword and with famine and with plague" in Revelation 6:8.
- So the Greek-level check does not confirm either Jeremiah 51 echo. They rest on English wording and shared rare words.
- I could not find an echo for other well-known Babylon parallels, such as "Babylon is fallen". The tables only list runs that appear in few verses, so those phrases may be too common to show up.
- The page open in the Reader (Ezra) has nothing to do with this question. The figures above all come from the Revelation pages.
